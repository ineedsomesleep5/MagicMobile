# C4: engine size and high-tier AI

Cloud session C4 of the game improvement program (docs/GAME_IMPROVEMENT_PROGRAM.md, Phases 3 and 4), for
R2. Branch `cloud/c4-engine-size-ai`, cut from `origin/cloud/c2-engine-a` (`41f123f`), with C3's calibration

Environment: MagicMobile Cloud (JDK 21, Maven 3.9.11, 4 CPUs, 15 GB). The container can't download

measurement-only workflow instead.

## Summary

- **Engine size.** The 202 MB of code is now broken down by owner from the shipped library's symbols:
  card classes 58%, set classes 14%, our generated card factory 9%, JDK 6% (TLS and crypto about 3.5 MB),
  H2 1.6%. One safe cut is implemented: a table-driven card factory, estimated 8–15 MB. Larger cuts are
  proposed with sizes and risks in docs/cloud/engine-size.md. The heap needs one opt-in native build to
  measure (added, off by default).
- **AI.** C3's finding has a cause: XMage's search is limited by time, not by its node cap, and attacks and
  blocks are fixed rules it never searches. A new expert tier (skill 5 and 6) plans combat, blocks as a
  whole, scores life on Commander's scale and keeps a quick move when the full search runs out of time.
  **Skill 6 now beats skill 3 in 62% of 317 decided games (95% CI 57–67%)**; on C2's engine it was 53%.
- All real-JVM tests pass on the final source, including save/resume checkpoints.

## Part 1: engine size

Details, tables and the full list of proposals: docs/cloud/engine-size.md.

- **No build report exists for this toolchain.** GraalVM 22.1 has no `--emit build-report` and no `-Os`,
  and Gluon's cross-compiles exit (`-H:+ExitAfterRelocatableImageWrite`) before native-image prints its size
  table.
- **Code, measured** (Android run 37683328246, `0546f91`, 257,057 function symbols, 202.0 MB): card classes
  117.7 MB (constructors 66.5, `copy` 28.6), set classes 27.7 MB, generated card factory 18.7 MB, JDK 11.2 MB,
  rest of XMage 14.2 MB, H2 3.2 MB, GraalVM stubs and runtime 4.0 MB, protobuf 1.3 MB. Tools: new workflow
  `.github/workflows/magicmobile-native-code-sizes.yml` (about a minute, builds nothing) and
  `packages/ondevice-engine/scripts/native_code_sizes.py`.
- **Heap, partly known**: at least 34 MB of vtables, 8.6 MB of resources, reflection and serialization
  registration for 52,566 classes; the rest needs the heap histogram.
- **Run-time memory found**: the set registry the engine installs at start-up keeps 93,420 `SetCardInfo`
  objects, 24.1 MB of Java heap on the JVM (10.5 s to install on a cold JVM), data the embedded catalogue
  already holds.
- **Implemented**: table-driven generated card factory (`engine/tools/RegistryExporter.java`). Each of
  the 256 buckets binary-searches a sorted name table and switches on an int, with one `CardSetInfo` check
  per bucket, instead of a string switch with an inlined `String.equals` and a throw site per card.
  Bytecode 4.55 to 3.61 MB; estimated 8–15 MB of native code. Same catalogue and registry hash.
- **Proposed, not implemented** (each needs a native build to confirm): sets from the catalogue (up to
  27.7 MB code and about 24 MB heap), H2 and ORMLite JDBC unreachable (about 3.5 MB), no TLS (up to
  3.5 MB), protobuf out of checkpoint registration (up to 1.3 MB), locales and charsets. Cuts 1–5 together
  come to roughly 45–50 MB of code. Card classes stay: no pruning (BUILD_AND_DEVICE_GATES.md).
- **Opt-in heap report**: input `native_reports` on `magicmobile-android.yml` (`MM_NATIVE_REPORTS=1` in
  `scripts/android/build_native.sh`) adds `-H:+PrintHeapHistogram`, `-H:+PrintImageHeapPartitionSizes`,
  `-H:+PrintClassInitialization` and a dashboard dump (heap and code per class and method) to the evidence
  artifact. Off by default; the library is unchanged either way.

## Part 2: stronger AI at skill 5 and 6

### What limits the AI today

- **Not the node cap.** Search stats from calibration games: a median of 12–18 nodes per search (90th
  percentile about 600) against the 5000 cap. Each node copies the whole Commander game, about 20 ms, so
  the time budget ends the search first. About 2% of searches ran out of time before scoring any move, and
  the AI then passed its main phase.
- **Attacks and blocks are fixed rules.** Upstream attacks only with creatures no single blocker can kill,
  and blocks each attacker on its own.
- **Life is scored for 20-life formats.** Upstream values a point of life at 100 above 20 life. At
  Commander's 40, a skill 6 AI let five 5/3 goblins through and fell from 40 to 15, then lost (replay of
  calibration game 32).

### What changed (mobile AI layer only, no upstream edits)

`AIDifficulty.expert` is true from skill 5 (Diamond). Expert AIs:

1. **Plan attacks** (`CombatPlanner.planAttack`): every subset of available attackers (hill-climbing above
   ten) is scored against the blocks the defender is predicted to make and the crack-back of the
   defender's whole team next turn, in upstream's own permanent values. With several opponents,
   attackable planeswalkers or battles, infect, or a defender who can't lose, upstream decides (5% of
   expert attacks in calibration).
2. **Choose blocks together** (`CombatPlanner.planBlocks`): hill-climbing over single and pair moves (double
   blocks, menace) on attackers killed, blockers lost and the life change. Upstream blocks when an attack
   targets a planeswalker or battle, involves infect, or when the engine asks again after a requirement
   check.
3. **Score life on the game's scale** (`CombatPlanner.lifeValue`): upstream's curve with life scaled by
   20 / starting life (identical at 20).
4. **Keep a quick move** (`MobileAICancellation.expertSearch`): a two-ply search (at most 2 s) before the
   full one; when the full search runs out of time before scoring any move, the quick move stands instead
   of a pass. The total stays within the think budget.

Think time is unchanged (`skill × 3` s). The flag is saved with checkpoints like the other difficulty
settings. PROTOCOL.md describes the expert tier.

### Memory

- Heap (JVM, serial GC, 768 MB cap, two games each on the same seeds): skill 6 vs 6 peaked at 273 and
  419 MB after collections; skill 4 vs 4 at 263 and 316 MB. Both AIs search; the difference follows game
  length and upstream's depth 6. The expert's own additions are small: combat arrays, plus one quick
  two-ply search tree held while the full search runs. No game ran out of memory under 768 MB.
- One calibration game ran out of a 2560 MB worker heap (above). Its stack shows XMage's own search on a
  large board after a timeout; C3 saw the same on today's engine. Worth watching on phones regardless of
  this change.

## Not verified

- No native build, device or simulator. The card factory's native saving is an estimate until the next
  Android build; the heap breakdown needs `native_reports`.
- AI strength was measured AI against AI on the cloud JVM. A phone's native engine is slower or faster per
  node, which changes how often searches time out; combat decisions don't depend on time.
- Calibration plays expert against upstream-style AIs. Against people, the planner's model of the defender
  (upstream's blocks plus chumps at lethal) is an approximation.

## What the apps must change

Nothing in the protocol: keep sending only `aiSkill`. Diamond (5) and Mythic (6) now play as experts.

1. Update any player-facing text that says Platinum to Mythic play alike, or that tiers differ only by
   decks.
2. Engine build identity changes with every engine release (as with C2): a game saved on R1 answers
   `checkpoint_incompatible` after the update, and the apps already offer to abandon it.

   consider `native_reports: true` on the Android build, then `magicmobile-native-code-sizes.yml` on its

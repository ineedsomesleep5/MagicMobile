## Precon round-robin (Bronze decks, today's engine)

Every pair of the five precons, both AIs at skill 3, phone budget: 20 games per pair (each deck goes
first in 10), so 80 games per precon, 200 in all. Seed 20261015. All 200 games ended normally (no turn
or time limits). Average 18.2 turns, 38 s per game. Data: `docs/cloud/c3-results/precon-round-robin.csv`.

| Precon | Commander | Wins–losses | Win rate (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| draconic-destruction | Atarka, World Render | 50–30 | 62% (52–72) |
| first-flight | Isperia, Supreme Judge | 45–35 | 56% (45–67) |
| token-triumph | Emmara, Soul of the Accord | 40–40 | 50% (39–61) |
| grave-danger | Gisa and Geralf | 36–44 | 45% (35–56) |
| chaos-incarnate | Kardur, Doomscourge | 29–51 | 36% (27–47) |

Interval: Wilson score, 95%. Only the two ends separate clearly: Draconic Destruction is the strongest
precon in the AI's hands and Chaos Incarnate the weakest (it won 3 of 20 against Draconic Destruction).
The middle three overlap. Going first won 130 of 200 games (65%, 95% CI 58–71), which is a bigger effect
than any precon's.

Command: `python3 scripts/ai_calibration.py run --skill-a 3 --skill-b 3 --pool precon --games 200`, then
`summarize --by-deck`.

## What changed

- `packages/ondevice-engine/tools/ai-calibration/AICalibration.java`: plays AI-vs-AI games on the real
  JVM engine with the production adapter classes. Two budgets, `phone` (default) and `watcher` (the
  October method, a human watcher seat that concedes at once). See the README there.
- `packages/ondevice-engine/scripts/ai_calibration.py`: resolves the ranked AI deck pools (5 precons
  from PreconCatalog.swift, 21 decks from ai-decks.json), schedules seeded games (both deck assignments
  and both seatings per deck pair), runs them on one JVM per CPU, writes a CSV per pairing (winner,
  turns, seconds), resumes after an interruption and prints Wilson 95% intervals, per pairing or per
  deck (`summarize --by-deck`). It also passes C2's per-seat `aiSearchDepth` and `aiMistakePercent`
  overrides and records each AI's deliberate-mistake count, on engines that have them.
- `tools/ai-calibration/baseline-matrix.json`: skill 1 vs 3, 2 vs 4, 3 vs 4 and 1–5 vs 6, 80 games each,
  on the eleven Core-bracket decks (precons plus Core AI decks), so skill is the only difference.

## Finding before any numbers: the October calibration measured the wrong budget

When the watcher seat concedes, no person is left in the game, and `MobileAICancellation.thinkBudget`
caps every AI search at 2 s (`STACK_THINK_SECS`, "watching pace"). So the October 6–6 for skill 6 vs 2
compared two AIs that both had 2 s, not 6 s against 18 s. Against a person on a phone the AI keeps its
full `skill × 3` s on an empty stack. The harness's default `phone` budget reproduces that: the two AIs
use a `MobileAICancellation` that is never bound to the live game, so `humansPlaying()` stays true. The
`watcher` budget is kept to compare with October.

Upstream XMage also stops a search at 5000 nodes (`ComputerPlayer6.MAX_SIMULATED_NODES_PER_CALC`), so
more time only helps until that cap; the smoke games show searches still time out at skill 1 and 6
("AI player thinks too long" warnings), so time does bind at these budgets on this CPU.

## Baseline on today's engine (origin/main `7cda863`, phone budget, Core pool)

640 games: 80 per pairing, on the eleven Core-bracket decks (5 precons and 6 Core AI decks), every deck
pair with both deck assignments and both seatings. Seed 20261015. All 640 ended normally (no turn or time
limits). Lower skill is A. Data: `docs/cloud/c3-results/baseline/`.

| Pairing | Lower skill's win rate (95% CI) | Avg turns | Avg s/game |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 vs 3 | 58% (47–68) | 16.0 | 36 |
| 2 vs 4 | 51% (40–62) | 16.2 | 37 |
| 3 vs 4 | 52% (42–63) | 16.8 | 54 |
| 1 vs 6 | 50% (39–61) | 16.1 | 60 |
| 2 vs 6 | 62% (52–72) | 16.3 | 48 |
| 3 vs 6 | 50% (39–61) | 16.6 | 63 |
| 4 vs 6 | 56% (45–67) | 16.0 | 61 |
| 5 vs 6 | 51% (40–62) | 15.8 | 62 |

- **Today's skill setting does not make the AI stronger.** In no pairing does the higher skill win
  more. Pooled over all 640 games the lower skill won 54% (50–58), and skills 1–5 together went 216–184
  against skill 6 (54%, 49–59). Skill 2 even beat skill 6 (62%, 52–72). The October 6–6 result was not
  just a watcher-budget artefact: with the full phone budget, skill 6's extra think time and depth
  (6 against 4) still buy nothing measurable, likely because the search stops at 5000 nodes anyway. A
  deeper tree under the same node cap is shallower in breadth, which may even cost a little.
- **Going first matters more than skill.** The first player won 387 of 640 (60%, 57–64); in the precon
  round-robin, 65%.
- So today Bronze through Mythic all face the same AI strength; only the decks differ by tier.

## C2's engine (`cloud/c2-engine-a` `41f123f`), same matrix

C2's defaults: skill 1 searches depth 2 with 15% mistakes, skill 2 depth 3 with 10%, skill 3 depth 4
with 5%, and skill 4 and up are unchanged. Same 640 scheduled games (seed, decks, seatings) as the
baseline. Run on an adapter-only build: C2's `engine/xmage` compiled over this checkout's upstream,
core and platform classes, which C2 does not change. All 640 games ended; 2 were real draws (both
players lost at once). Data: `docs/cloud/c3-results/c2/`.

| Pairing | Today: lower skill wins | C2: lower skill wins (95% CI) | C2 mistakes/game (lower) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 vs 3 | 58% | **29% (20–39)** | 3.6 (skill 3: 1.1) |
| 1 vs 6 | 50% | **27% (18–37)** | 3.4 |
| 2 vs 4 | 51% | **36% (27–47)** | 1.8 |
| 2 vs 6 | 62% | 40% (30–51) | 2.0 |
| 3 vs 4 | 52% | 46% (36–57) | 1.0 |
| 3 vs 6 | 50% | 47% (36–58) | 0.8 |
| 4 vs 6 | 56% | 50% (39–61) | – |
| 5 vs 6 | 51% | 49% (38–60) | – |

Bold: the interval excludes 50%, so the lower skill is measurably weaker.

- **Bronze and Silver now work.** Skill 1 won 28% of its games against 3 and 6 (21–35% pooled), and
  skill 2 won 38% against 4 and 6 (31–46%). Both were at or above 50% on today's engine.
- **Gold is not yet measurably easier than Platinum.** Skill 3 won 46–47% (39–54% pooled). Its 5% rate
  gives under one mistake a game.
- **Platinum to Mythic play alike** (49–50%), as the program intends ("full strength from Platinum
  up"): those tiers differ by deck bracket only. Mistakes per game are about 3.5 at 15% and 2 at 10%
  because only some decisions roll (a search that chose to act, attacks, blocks).
- The first player won 399 of 640 (62%).

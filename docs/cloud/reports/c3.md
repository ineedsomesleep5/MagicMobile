## What changed

- `packages/ondevice-engine/tools/ai-calibration/AICalibration.java`: plays AI-vs-AI games on the real
  JVM engine with the production adapter classes. Two budgets, `phone` (default) and `watcher` (the
  October method, a human watcher seat that concedes at once). See the README there.
- `packages/ondevice-engine/scripts/ai_calibration.py`: resolves the ranked AI deck pools (5 precons
  from PreconCatalog.swift, 21 decks from ai-decks.json), schedules seeded games (both deck assignments
  and both seatings per deck pair), runs them on one JVM per CPU, writes a CSV per pairing (winner,
  turns, seconds), resumes after an interruption and prints Wilson 95% intervals, per pairing or per
  deck (`summarize --by-deck`). It also passes C2's per-seat `aiSearchDepth` and `aiMistakePercent`
  overrides and records each AI's deliberate-mistake count, on engines that have them.
- `tools/ai-calibration/baseline-matrix.json`: skill 1 vs 3, 2 vs 4, 3 vs 4 and 1–5 vs 6, 80 games each,
  on the eleven Core-bracket decks (precons plus Core AI decks), so skill is the only difference.

## Finding before any numbers: the October calibration measured the wrong budget

When the watcher seat concedes, no person is left in the game, and `MobileAICancellation.thinkBudget`
caps every AI search at 2 s (`STACK_THINK_SECS`, "watching pace"). So the October 6–6 for skill 6 vs 2
compared two AIs that both had 2 s, not 6 s against 18 s. Against a person on a phone the AI keeps its
full `skill × 3` s on an empty stack. The harness's default `phone` budget reproduces that: the two AIs
use a `MobileAICancellation` that is never bound to the live game, so `humansPlaying()` stays true. The
`watcher` budget is kept to compare with October.

Upstream XMage also stops a search at 5000 nodes (`ComputerPlayer6.MAX_SIMULATED_NODES_PER_CALC`), so
more time only helps until that cap; the smoke games show searches still time out at skill 1 and 6
("AI player thinks too long" warnings), so time does bind at these budgets on this CPU.
